In the book "Atomic Habits", I found an especially surprising finding.

\_\_ insert quote \_\_

If you want to improve, the most important thing is practice. But you can't improve your practice if you're not practicing.

Establishing a quantity of practice is the first step to being able to quickly iterate and improve on the quality of that practice.

To become a great X, do X every day. Reflect on X, modify X, and you will soon become great at X.

The best part is that much of this happens automatically by just establishing the quantity. We naturally notice things we could improve on as we practice, and most of our adjustments initially do not require intentional practice.
