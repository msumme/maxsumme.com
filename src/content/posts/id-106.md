In my job as a software developer and consultant, getting a "No" is not an uncommon experience. Not getting the occasional "No" probably means you're not pushing yourself beyond your comfort zone or going after ambitious projects.\\ \\ But how do you handle losing the contract you thought was in the bag? How do you handle the unexpected disappointments? How do you keep these setbacks from causing your confidence to flag?\\ \\ Sometimes, enough things are going well that a few unexpected setbacks don't affect your overall attitude.\\ \\ When you are in the thick of it, and you really need things to break your way, small setbacks can feel devastating. They can seem to knock you down and keep you there.\\ \\ What should you do?\\ \\ If you can counteract mentally by putting the rejection in context, or reframing it so that it is not a rejection, that is best.\\ \\ Sometimes, however, you may be too tired or otherwise out of fighting spirit, and it will get you. At this point, the easiest way past it is through it. Just mourn. Let yourself feel bad. Write in a journal. Let the sorrow wash over you. Sit with it for a while. Let it run its course.\\ \\ Getting rejected is not an easy thing. When it starts to feel personal (even though it usually is not) you need to take time to let those emotions resolve themselves. You need to let yourself process them without reacting or doing anything particularly stupid. And you need to guard against letting yourself become embittered or angry.\\ \\ Finally, once this is over, it is time to begin again building yourself from the inside, starting with your beliefs.\\ \\ I can win. I can improve. I am getting better.\\ \\ Remind yourself to keep trying. Maybe this downside is an upside. Some clients are horrible to work with. Other times, it may just be an opportunity to grow and develop.\\ \\ Opportunities are often disguised as hard work. But they can also be disguised as other unpleasantness.
