Software has undoubtedly made everyone's lives more convenient, if by convenient you mean fast shipping and being able to find out what song is playing at the bar you're visiting.

But there is a hidden cost to everything that software (or anything else) does for you.

It weakens your agency. It disempowers you. It makes you forget how. It makes you dependent on "the experts"

You used to need to remember a lot of facts and figures. Memorization was a typical part of school. Now we just [google things](https://www.wired.com/2011/07/is-google-ruining-your-memory/).

This is inevitable, as our minds are highly adaptive, and everything we learn how to do the easy way, we eventually forget how to do it the hard way.

Eventually, self-driving cars may mean people don't know how to get around on their own. And eventually there may be no option to actually drive your own car. And nobody will know how, except for some software developers somewhere.

When we let experts decide things for us, we let once what was common knowledge be siphoned out of the communal memory. We all agree to let someone else do that part of our thinking for us.

In the case of self-driving cars, we are trusting some company (and specifically its programmers) to make a dizzying range of practical and ethical decisions.

Not to mention that the infrastructure we are building could very easily be modified to take your ability to get from place to place away, as OTA updates are becoming the norm for updating software in advanced automobiles.

At that point, control over basic movement becomes not only difficult, but perhaps even impossible for anyone not approved by the powerful interests (whether it be corporations or governments).

Not to be all doom and gloom though, perhaps the DMV would be a thing of the past.
