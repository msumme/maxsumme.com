One of my biggest challenges with this blog is building an email list.  I've never done it before.  I know it doesn't have to be hard, but the idea of asking people I know and/or have worked with in the past to join an email list wherein the only thing they get for their efforts is email content from yours truly - it feels like I have eaten the Sandwich of Embarrassment.\\ \\ Unfortunately, I have gotten stuck on this step.  This is a post about the things I am trying in order to get myself past The Fear™ and into action.\\ \\ If you aren't the kind of person who likes to know How the Sausage Gets Made or have an exceptionally and irrationally high opinion of me (unlikely, I know) maybe don't read the rest of this post.\\

## \#1 Do something drastic

\\ So I sent a fairly self-deprecating email to a couple of people just to see what response I would get.  It actually worked - I got some sign-ups - basically everyone who got an email.  But it still didn't feel right.  I still had the nagging feeling that I was commercializing friendships, which is something I desperately want to avoid doing.  I like my friends, and I want to keep them, and I don't want to put a strain on relationships if it can be avoided.\\

## \#2 Immediately send said people messages asking them for feedback

\\ Given the horrible feeling experienced upon hitting send, I sent these people messages asking for feedback specifically on the copy of the email, to see how it came across.  I thought initially it was funny, but now that I have done this, and felt this discomfort in my stomach, I wanted to climb into deep pit and hide forever.\\

## \#3 Revamp messaging

\\ This totally sucks too, I am not going to send this.\\

## \#4 Come Up With A Self-Bribery System

\\ I decided that the only way past something I don't want to do is a bigger carrot of something I do want.\\ \\ I deeply and truly love cheese burgers.  They are perhaps one of the Perfect Foods.  I am particularly in love with In'N'Out.\\ \\ So I set milestones.  For 25, 50, 100, and 200 subscribers, I am giving myself a trip to In'N'Out.  Hell, I'll even bring the whole family.\\ \\ For 500 and 1000 subs, not sure what the reward system will be yet.  It should be perhaps bigger.  I will have to figure this out.  Maybe a short trip somewhere with the wife - those are definitely going to be stretch goals.\\

## \#5 Accept said self-bribe and just message everybody individually

\\ This is maybe the thing I feared the most.  Admitting I don't know what I'm doing to people who know me, and asking for their help.\\ \\ At the end of the day, this is really what the fear is about.  It's about rejection.  Who is going to say NO to me today?   What if I lose friendships trying something new?\\ \\ What if I let people see vulnerability and they hate it / me?  What if they lose their good opinion of me?\\ \\ And yet, if I don't actually get around to opening up to other people, connections can't happen.  Chance can't occur.  Beautiful ideas won't develop, and partnerships won't form.\\ \\ So I just started sending people a message that was honest about that.  "I don't know what I'm doing and trying to figure it out, can you help me by joining this email list and maybe sharing stuff if you think it's good"  - that was basically the message.  So far - good results.\\ \\ Everybody has been supportive, even if reluctantly (how can you tell if someone secretly hates you over a FB message?), and nobody has told me they hate me, unfriended me, or otherwise told me to go to hell.\\

## Conclusion

\\ The fear appears to have been mostly unfounded.  It was just another mental block that made a relatively easy thing (typing messages to 50+ people) a relatively impossible thing in my mind.\\ \\ So far, up to 26 subscribers.  It is a small victory, but I get to go to In'N'Out, which in my book is a major win.\\ \\ But the most important piece of information gained is this - **bribing yourself works.  Do it liberally.**\\ \\ Discipline is better, but doing something is better than not doing it just because you can't be perfect.
