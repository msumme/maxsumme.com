So many companies operate under the assumption that the solution to their people problems is to limit their people.

In *The E-Myth Revisited*, the author says you should make a System of Systems so your business doesn't rely on people's skills.

This is not an entirely wrong idea. Systems are necessary, and powerful, and can unlock huge amounts of productivity. I would never advocate against systems as such.

But systems should not stifle your people, or remove their value.

"But if I let my people make decisions, they might make mistakes!"

If you don't let your people make decisions, how human is their work? How much do they show up? How much do they show you what they can do?

Systems are there to do things well that people are not good at doing consistently. To remove toil so we can focus on higher value-creating work.

## Generative versus repetitive work

If you hire people to create things, they need to exercise their judgment constantly. This is very different than hiring someone to be part of a machine (assembly lines, working in completely constrained systems where only effort is needed, not judgement).

Creative work requires autonomy.

I would argue we need fewer businesses like McDonald's or Walmart in the world. They have their benefits, and have made certain things easier or cheaper. But the costs are hidden.

When you unlock people, and train them, they add value.

If your systems are limiting your people's growth, you are losing value.

If your systems are removing their ability to make mistakes, you are losing value.

If you are trying to eliminate risk with your systems in a way that also eliminates the power of your employees, you're losing value.

What if you instead trusted your employees to design the system that controls their outputs?

But what if instead of asking "How can systems make people less essential?" you asked "What if systems helped you get maximum value from your people, and turned your people into a competitive advantage?"

If your people are a liability, you are hiring wrong, training wrong, leading wrong, and probably have poor systems as well.

What would you have to do to unlock maximum value from your employees? How would you have to lead to make them come fully alive, and show up for your customers?
